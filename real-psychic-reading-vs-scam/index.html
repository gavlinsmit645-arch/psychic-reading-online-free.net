How to Tell a Real Psychic Reading From a Scam: A Sceptic’s Guide

March 12, 2026 · By David Okafor · 3 min read

The psychic industry has a trust problem. For every genuine reader delivering real value, there are operators exploiting vulnerable people with fake readings, manipulative tactics, and financial traps. If you’re sceptical about psychic readings, that scepticism is well-earned.

But here’s the thing: scepticism and open-mindedness aren’t mutually exclusive. You can be cautious and curious at the same time. The key is knowing what to look for – and what to run from.

Red Flags: Signs of a Scam

They Demand Payment Upfront for a “Free” Reading

The most common tactic is offering a “free” reading that requires your credit card “for verification.” This is not free. It’s a bait-and-switch designed to charge you later, often through hidden recurring subscriptions.

A genuinely free reading requires no payment information at all. If someone asks for your card before you’ve received anything, walk away.

They Use Fear and Urgency

Scam readers create fear to drive spending. They might tell you there’s a “dark energy” around you, a “curse” on your family, or a “negative entity” attached to you – and that only they can remove it, for a fee.

Genuine readers don’t use fear as a sales tool. They provide insight and guidance without manufactured urgency or threat.

They Claim 100% Accuracy

No psychic is 100% accurate. Genuine readers acknowledge the limitations of their ability and the inherent uncertainty of reading energy and potential futures. Anyone who guarantees absolute accuracy is either deluded or dishonest.

They Ask Leading Questions

Cold reading is a technique where the reader asks questions, watches your reactions, and feeds back information you’ve unknowingly provided. “I’m sensing someone close to you has passed… someone older… a father figure, maybe?” – this is fishing, not reading.

A genuine psychic provides information without asking for it. They tell you things; they don’t extract them.

They Offer Vague, Universal Statements

“I sense you’ve been through some difficult times recently.” Who hasn’t? Statements so vague that they apply to virtually anyone are not evidence of psychic ability. They’re evidence of psychological manipulation.

Green Flags: Signs of a Genuine Reader

They Provide Specific Information

A genuine reader will offer details that are specific to you – names, situations, dynamics, timings. The information should make you think “how could they know that?” rather than “that could apply to anyone.”

They’re Comfortable Being Wrong

Good readers acknowledge when they’re off-target. They’ll say “I’m not sure about this one” or “Does this make sense to you?” rather than insisting every impression is correct.

They Don’t Use Fear or Pressure

A genuine reading feels empowering, not frightening. You should leave feeling clearer, not more anxious. If a reader makes you feel scared and then offers to “fix” the problem for a price, that’s manipulation.

They Respect Your Autonomy

A real reader offers guidance and perspective – they don’t tell you what to do or create dependency. They empower your decision-making rather than replacing it.

They Have Transparent Pricing

If there are costs involved, they should be clearly stated upfront with no surprises. And “free” should genuinely mean free.

How to Test a Reading’s Quality

After receiving a reading, ask yourself these questions:

1. Did the reader provide specific information I hadn’t shared?

2. Did they make me feel empowered rather than fearful?

3. Was the guidance actionable and relevant to my actual situation?

4. Was I pressured to spend money at any point?

5. Do I feel clearer about my situation than before?

If you answered yes to most of these, you’ve likely experienced a genuine reading. If you answered no, trust that instinct.

The existence of scams doesn’t negate the existence of genuine psychic ability. It simply means you need to approach the field with intelligence and discernment. The best psychic readings are those where you walked in sceptical and walked out impressed – not by theatrics, but by truth.
